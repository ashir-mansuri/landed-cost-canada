    Incoterms 2020: who pays for what

    An Incoterm in a quote decides which costs are already in the supplier’s price and which ones you pay on top. The calculator uses this table for each supplier.

    C-terms (CFR, CPT, CIF, CIP) are read here as delivery to a Canadian port or terminal. D-terms (DAP, DPU, DDP) are read as delivery to your warehouse. Under E, F and some C-terms, insurance is your choice; leave it at zero if you don’t insure.

    How the numbers work
    • Value for duty is the supplier’s price in Canadian dollars, plus any costs to get the goods to the place they are shipped to Canada from. Transport after that point isn’t charged duty. When a C- or D-term price already includes freight, insurance or delivery, your estimate of those costs is taken out before duty is worked out. To claim this with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), you need documents showing the actual transport costs.
    • Duty is the value for duty times the duty rate. A surtax or counter-tariff works the same way and is added on top.
    • GST is 5% of the value for duty plus duty and surtax. It only counts toward landed cost when you switch it on in step 1.
    • Landed cost is the supplier’s price plus every charge you pay under the Incoterm, plus duty and surtax (and GST if included). Per unit is that total divided by the order quantity.
    • CO₂ is the shipment weight in tonnes times distance times a typical factor: sea 0.016, rail 0.026, truck 0.11 and air 0.65 kg CO₂e per tonne-km. These are rounded from UK government greenhouse-gas conversion factors and are for comparison only.
    • Scores. Landed cost, lead time and CO₂ are scored against the best supplier, which gets 10. A supplier costing 25% more scores 8. Quality, delivery reliability and supply risk use your own ratings. A missing lead time or distance scores a neutral 5.
    • Exchange rates come from the Bank of Canada’s daily rates when you press “Get today’s rates”. If that service can’t be reached, the European Central Bank’s reference rates are used instead.

    Find duty rates in the CBSA Customs Tariff or the Canada Tariff Finder. CBSA’s guide Importing commercial goods: determining duties and taxes explains the official method.
